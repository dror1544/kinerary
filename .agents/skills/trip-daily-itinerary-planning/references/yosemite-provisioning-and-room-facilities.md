# Yosemite provisioning and room facilities example

Use for questions like: “Tomorrow on the way, can we stop at a good supermarket to stock up? Can we cook in the hotel room?”

## Pattern

1. Identify the next lodging from bookings/trip plan.
   - In this session the next lodging after San Francisco was **Yosemite View Lodge, El Portal**.
2. Treat room food facilities as a fact to verify, not infer.
   - If the confirmation or reliable hotel page cannot be read, avoid saying “you can cook.”
   - Safer wording: “Plan for light meals, storage, and reheating unless we verify a stove/kitchenette/cookware.”
3. Route-aware grocery recommendation:
   - For SF → Yosemite View Lodge, the full-supermarket stop should be before the remote Yosemite/El Portal area.
   - Verify **"on the way / no detour"** with routing, not intuition: compare direct hotel→lodging drive time against hotel→supermarket and supermarket→lodging.
   - In this session, **Grocery Outlet, 1125 W Main St, Merced** was confirmed as a practical stop on the Hwy 140 approach, adding only about **3 minutes** versus the direct route.
   - Oakdale can also be a practical grocery town on Yosemite-bound routes, but do not hard-code it as the default; choose the town that actually matches tomorrow's route.
   - Groveland has more limited market options; El Portal/park-area supplies are typically less convenient and more expensive.
4. Suggested shopping list when cooking facilities are uncertain:
   - Water, fruit, bread/tortillas, cheese/members, yogurt, cereal/granola, snacks, sandwich supplies.
   - Microwave-friendly items only if microwave is confirmed or likely.
   - Avoid buying raw ingredients that require stove/oven/cookware unless the room facilities are verified.

## Pitfall

Do not overstate “there is a kitchenette” from memory or from generic hotel assumptions. Separate verified room amenities from practical advice about stocking up before Yosemite.
